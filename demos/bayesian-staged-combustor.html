What to look for

First, four tests spread across the input space. Then the Bayesian model fills in between measurements. The next point seeks information; it can have higher emissions or pressure than the last one.

How this relates to the real test

The real app models NO, not total NOx (NO + NO₂). This demo follows that NO channel. Confirm the analyser species before interpreting a result as NOx.

Pressure here is the dominant spectral amplitude in Pa RMS, not supply pressure or mean chamber pressure. Each transducer has a separate model.

Real observations use averaged measured flows, eligible dry NO/O₂ samples, and matched LabVIEW TDMS recordings. You verify calibration, reporting basis, steady conditions and the operating region before completing a trial.

These bounds, flows and responses are illustrative. They do not establish a safe burner envelope or a safe transition between settings.

Why this point?

The initial design gives the model a first set of observations. It is coverage, not an attempt to find a low-NO point.

Bayesian process in plain language
  1. Observe: record one settled operating point and its responses.
  2. Update: fit separate Gaussian processes. Each predicts a mean response and its uncertainty.
  3. Choose: estimate how much each candidate would reduce uncertainty across the input space.
  4. Repeat: review one proposal, measure it and update the maps.

Score = w × NO information gain + (1 − w) / 2 × (P1 gain + P2 gain). Gains are fractional variance reductions, so ppm and Pa are not added together.

This browser demo uses a deterministic Halton candidate pool and fixed Matérn-5/2 length scales and noise. The real controller uses Sobol pools and fits kernel parameters and pressure noise from the observations. The demo performs actual GP conditioning and integrated variance reduction, with simplified statistics.
